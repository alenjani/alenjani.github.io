Production recsys
A two-tower recommender that has to answer in 10 milliseconds

InfoNCE + same-taxonomy hard negatives, ONNX serving, HNSW retrieval. The lift came from the negatives, not the architecture.

+

One tower for users, one for items, both trained with InfoNCE — the textbook setup. The interesting part was the hard negatives: random negatives let the model coast on macro categories ("food vs. household"), which looks great on offline metrics and adds nothing online. Sampling negatives from the same taxonomy node (Greek yogurt vs. plain yogurt) forced fine-grained attribute discrimination, and the lift came from there.

Serving was the other half. ONNX over PyTorch for the user tower because the PyTorch graph had branches the runtime didn't need. HNSW for the item index — the recall/latency frontier on a couple hundred thousand items lets you get sub-10ms p99 if the index is warm and the network isn't lying to you.

Training compute went sideways before it went down. MosaicML Streaming for sharded reads on 8× A100s fixed the I/O bottleneck. Then the bigger win: a bi-weekly warm-start strategy. Embeddings drift slowly, and you don't need to relearn that yogurt is dairy every two weeks.

Evaluation · LLM
An LLM judge for the question offline metrics can't answer

Precision@k can't tell you if a cart of 8 items hangs together. A CoT judge across 7 latent dimensions can.

+

Precision@k tells you whether the items are individually relevant. It can't tell you whether the bundle of 8 items hangs together as a coherent shopping cart. That's a semantic call a human can make in five seconds and offline metrics can't make at all.

So the judge: an LLM scoring orthogonal dimensions with Chain-of-Thought — complementarity, taxonomy coherence, occasion fit, and a few others — calibrated against a small panel of human raters. It isn't replacing the panel. It's letting the panel's judgment run across millions of cart variants per release as a quality gate. The interesting design choice was making the prompts declarative, so the dimensions can be audited, swapped, or retired independently without reshipping the whole evaluator.

Simulation · counterfactual
An offline simulator that's honest about online behavior

Time-travel sampling, LLM user agents, and metrics that adapt when sim disagrees with online.

+

The chronic problem in recsys is that offline metrics and online A/B tests rarely agree, and the gap eats your release cadence. The sim replays historical state ("time-travel sampling") and lets LLM-driven user agents react to a candidate model. The closing-the-loop part is the self-adaptive metric tuning: when sim disagreed with online last release, the metric weights shift toward whichever dimensions actually predicted the gap. It's not a perfect oracle. It's a faster wrong answer that gets less wrong over time.

Computer vision · industrial
CV on a plant floor that wouldn't stop lying to us

The model trained beautifully and collapsed in prod. The fix was decision logic, not architecture.

+

Multi-class object detection on a real production line. The model trained beautifully and collapsed in prod — false positives everywhere. Lighting changed station to station, parts drifted out of alignment, and most parts were fine which made the imbalance brutal.

The fix was unglamorous. Leakage-safe data pipeline on GCP/GCS with Label Studio so we stopped training on test-set parts. Hard negatives mined from the failure cases. Operational thresholds tuned against the actual cost of a false alarm vs. a missed defect — they're not symmetric. The model didn't get smarter. The decision logic around it did.

Disaster CV / built-environment risk
Eng. Structures · 2020
Pre-event and post-event imagery fusion

Faster R-CNN + depth-aware homography. Fuse Street View pre-event with drone post-event.

+

End-to-end pipeline. Input: a building's address. Output: a damage state. Google Street View for pre-event imagery, drone or satellite for post-event, Faster R-CNN to find the building in each frame, a depth model to disambiguate which of several visible buildings is the building, and a homography step to align them. The fusion is the part everyone underestimates — pre-event Street View is sharp but oblique; post-event drone is overhead and noisy; matching them requires a depth-aware registration that the obvious affine transform fails at.

Natural Hazards · 2020
Triaging post-disaster inspections as resource allocation

Constrained optimization, simulated annealing, and a Pareto frontier that turns triage into a decision rule.

+

Cities have finite inspectors and infinite buildings after a disaster. The status quo is intuition. We modeled it as constrained resource allocation: which inspections, in what order, restore the most community function per inspector-hour? Closed-form for small graphs, simulated annealing for real city-scale ones, with uncertainty propagated from the damage estimates.

CACAIE · 2019
Buildings out of 360° panoramas

Panorama-aware detection plus geometric reasoning. The piece that makes pre-event extraction work.

+

Panoramas are messy: multiple buildings per frame, occlusions, perspective distortion at the seams. The detector is panorama-aware — it reasons about which face of the sphere corresponds to which side of the street, so the same building doesn't get detected twice across the seam, and the geometry of the camera position constrains which detections are even plausible.

Eng. Structures · 2022
Stacking classifier outputs across views

Per-image probabilities → RC and M lists → iterative thresholding → final damage state.

+

Same problem as the 2020 fusion paper, harder data: the Subject of Interest arrives as a set of images, not one. Per-image classifiers produce class-conditional probabilities for two materials (RC and masonry), then a probability-list filter and an iterative thresholding scheme combine those into a single overall damage state.

MDPI Sensors · 2020
Localizing indoor reconnaissance photos onto building drawings

Structure-from-Motion plus a 2D projective transform onto the structural drawing.

+

Indoor images don't get clean GPS — the signal drops, multipaths, and you can't tell which room an image was shot in just from metadata. The pipeline runs Structure-from-Motion across a stream of "PathImgs" walked through the building, reconstructs a 3D point cloud, and then solves a 2D projective transform that maps the SfM coordinate frame onto the structural drawing. Once that map exists, every "InspImg" — the targeted close-ups engineers actually care about — is grounded against a drawing location.

Space habitat resilience & digital twin
AIAA · 2019
Resilience as passive + active capacity

Decompose resilience into what the system absorbs and what operators can do once it's hit.

+

How do you design for resilience without it being a vibes-based adjective? This paper splits the concept into two components: passive capacity (redundancy, margin, robust design) and active capacity (repair, reconfigure, mitigate). Then the case study runs at 90%, 63%, and 40% passive capacity to see how the trade space behaves.

ASME IDETC · 2022
Habitat design as portfolio management (CDCM)

Markov subsystem nodes, value metric, risk-return trade studies the way a portfolio manager runs them.

+

Each subsystem is a coarse input/output Markov node — power, structural, life support, environment, agent, intelligent health management — wired together into configurations. Disturbances drive state transitions; a reflexive health-management subsystem prioritizes recovery actions given limited robotic-agent availability. The output is a value metric decomposing system performance into mission-value and crew-value over the design lifecycle. Then trade studies run as risk-return scatter for every candidate configuration.

ASCE Earth & Space · 2021
Cyber-physical testing for habitat control loops

Real hardware-in-the-loop coupled to a simulated habitat through sensor/actuator interfaces.

+

Pure simulation is cheap but lies. Pure physical testing is expensive and slow. Cyber-physical testing splits the difference: real hardware sits in a test cell while the rest of the habitat is simulated in real time, coupled through sensor and actuator interfaces. The control law sees the physical hardware's actual response and the simulated environment reacts accordingly.

About

Mechanical engineer who took a left turn into ML and never went back. PhD at Purdue on decision-making under uncertainty in physical systems, then postdocs at Stanford on multimodal ML for global health, and NASA STRI / Purdue on a space-habitat digital twin. Different domains, same job: build something that has to make a fast decision on real data without any of the inputs being clean.

Lately the work is mostly recommender systems and the evaluation problem around them — what to actually measure, how to use LLMs as judges without fooling yourself, and how to keep offline simulators honest about online behavior. Based in the SF Bay Area.
